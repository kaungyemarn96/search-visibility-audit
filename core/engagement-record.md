# Engagement record

What to capture during an engagement so it can be proven, repeated and sold afterwards.

Fill this in **as you go**. Reconstructing it later does not work: the before-state is gone the moment you fix it, and it is the before-state that carries all the value.

This exists because of a real gap. A site restructure delivered years earlier had no before-and-after sitemap kept, so the strongest evidence of the most valuable deliverable in that engagement no longer exists. The work was done. The proof was not.

---

## Capture before touching anything

The before-state is unrecoverable once work starts. Everything in this section has a deadline of day one.

- [ ] **Full crawl or export of the current structure.** Sitemap, navigation, URL list. Whatever form the estate takes.
- [ ] **Screenshots of every page or screen in scope**, at both breakpoints.
- [ ] **Baseline scores.** Whatever the bundle measures. Record the tool, the date and the settings, not just the number.
- [ ] **Baseline analytics.** The metric set that will be reported against, pulled before any change lands.
- [ ] **The current copy**, saved verbatim. This becomes the *before* column of the change register.
- [ ] **What the client believes is wrong**, in their words, quoted. Often diverges from what is actually wrong. Both are findings.
- [ ] **A completed `stack.md`.** Blanks are findings.

## Capture during

- [ ] **Every decision with a reason.** Especially the ones overruled by the client. Six months on, nobody remembers whether a structure was chosen or conceded.
- [ ] **Every blocker, with who held it and how long.** This is what explains a timeline.
- [ ] **Anything the client supplied late or not at all.** Not for blame. It is the evidence behind the scope note in the next proposal.
- [ ] **Questions asked at the clarification gate**, and the answers. These become the intake questions for the next client in the same sector.
- [ ] **Hours against each deliverable.** Feeds the next fixed-price quote.
- [ ] **Anything built that was not scoped.** Either it becomes billable next time or it becomes a bundle feature. Both need it recorded.

## Capture at close

- [ ] **The after-state**, in the same form as the before. Same tool, same settings, same breakpoints. A comparison across two different methods is not a comparison.
- [ ] **Re-run the baseline scores.** Same date-stamping discipline.
- [ ] **The delta, honestly.** Including anything that moved the wrong way, and why.
- [ ] **A handover list**: what was delivered, where it lives, what is still open, who owns each open item.
- [ ] **What the client actually used** versus what was delivered. The gap between the two is the most useful product signal available.
- [ ] **A quotable line from the client**, asked for while they are still pleased.
- [ ] **Permission**, in writing, on what may be shown publicly, at what level of detail, and whether they can be named.

---

## The permission question, asked properly

Ask at close, not months later when you need a case study and the contact has moved on.

Three separate permissions, and clients grant them differently:

**Name.** May the organisation be named. **Numbers.** May specific results be published, or only ranges and directions. **Artifacts.** May a sitemap, a screenshot, a report page be shown, and does it need redacting.

A no to naming is usually a yes to everything else, described generically. That is still a usable case study, and it is what makes a confidential engagement sellable at all.

---

## Why this is a bundle deliverable, not admin

Handed to a client, this is a governance artifact: it tells their team what to keep when they run the next change themselves. Kept internally, it is what turns twelve engagements into a product instead of twelve memories.

The pattern holds either way. Capture the before-state, or the work becomes unprovable the moment it succeeds.
