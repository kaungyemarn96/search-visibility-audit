# Keyword gap matrix

Terms as rows, the site and its named competitors as columns. The shape is what makes the gap obvious: a row where every column is weak is an opening, and a row where one competitor is strong and everyone else is absent is a target.

---

## Competitors benchmarked

Name them, with why each is in the set. Include at least one that is not a direct competitor but ranks for the terms anyway. A general-interest site or an encyclopedia entry frequently holds a term that a specialist site should own, and that is the most winnable row on the sheet.

**Cell values.** `Yes` means a dedicated page. `Partial` means the topic is covered inside a page about something else. `No` means absent. Partial is the most actionable value on the sheet, because the content exists and only needs its own home.

## Recommended targets

Two or three, no more. For each, state the case in four parts:

1. **Volume.** Roughly, and say it is rough.
2. **Who holds it now**, and why they are beatable. Usually because they are generalists and the client is not.
3. **The specific advantage** the client has. Direct experience, proprietary data, being the subject itself.
4. **Whether assistants answer this question**, and what they currently say. A term that appears in answers is worth more than its search volume suggests, because owning it in search increasingly means owning it in the answer.

## Rules

Do not list fifty terms. A matrix nobody can act on is a spreadsheet.

Terms surfaced here go into the keyword bank, not into a standalone document. A one-off keyword study is stale the moment the next page publishes.

Re-run quarterly with the sweep. Competitor positions move.
