# Audit

Establish where the site actually stands, before anything is agreed or changed.

**Trigger.** Start of an engagement. Then annually.

**Inputs.** Live site access. Analytics and search console where they exist. The client's own statement of what they think is wrong. A completed `core/stack.md`.

---

## Steps

**1. Capture the before-state first.** Everything in the capture-before-touching-anything section of `engagement.md`. Once a fix lands, the evidence is gone, and the before-state is what the whole engagement is measured against.

**2. Crawl the live site.** Every page reachable from the navigation and the sitemap, plus anything in search console with impressions and no internal link.

**3. Grade the five categories** per `references/scoring-rubric.md`. Pair every grade with its single most important specific.

**4. Score the three dimensions** out of 75, and state a target after implementation with the deliverables that produce it.

**5. Build the keyword gap matrix** against named competitors. Keywords as rows, competitors as columns, with intent, difficulty and current owner. See `templates/keyword-gap-matrix.md`.

**6. Triage every finding** into Critical, High or Medium, and map each to a delivery week. A finding with no week attached is a complaint.

**7. Check the technical layer.** Core performance thresholds on mobile and desktop separately, image formats, render-blocking resources, redirect chains, canonical correctness, sitemap presence and whether robots references it.

**8. Write it up** per `templates/audit-report.md`.

---

## Gates

**Before quoting a fixed price**, confirm who applies CMS changes. A scope built on the assumption that the writer can edit templates, handed to a client whose developer owns them, is a scope that fails in week one.

**Before promising a target**, confirm the deliverables that produce it are in scope. Targets follow deliverables, never the reverse.

---

## Output

The audit report, the score dashboard, the keyword gap matrix, the triaged issue list, and an hour-budgeted deliverable table if this feeds a proposal.

## Done when

Every finding carries a severity, a week and an owner. Every grade carries a specific. Every target names the deliverables behind it. The before-state is captured and stored. The client can read the first page and know what the worst problem is.

---

## The commercial shape this produces

A fixed price for the first month against an hour-budgeted deliverable table, then a retainer for the recurring cycles. The audit is what makes the fixed price possible: the hours are estimated against findings that exist, rather than against a scope imagined in advance.

Where the audit itself is paid, it stands alone and the client owes nothing further. Where it is free, it is a proposal, and it should say so.
